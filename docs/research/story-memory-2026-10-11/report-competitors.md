**Research on the underlying techniques**
- **Facts that change over time:** Zep's Graphiti stores each fact with `valid_at`/`invalid_at`. A fact that is no longer true is marked invalid, never deleted, and an LLM pulls the dates while processing the text.
- **Whole-book prompts lose accuracy:** NovelQA (ICLR 2025) and NovelHopQA show accuracy falls as the novel gets longer and as answers need more steps across the book. That argues for sending summaries plus targeted passages, not the whole manuscript.
- **DeepSeek V4 prices conflict across sources.** One snapshot of first-party prices: Flash $0.14 in / $0.28 out per million tokens, Pro $1.74 / $3.48, with cache hits around 80% cheaper. An October 2026 guide reports separate peak and off-peak prices. Either way, a stable prompt prefix is the main cost lever.

**Takeaways for the redesign**
- No leading tool asks authors to tag scenes. The tools that work link mentions by names and aliases, which is exact, free and explainable, and use AI only for summaries and facts.
- MythScribe already has most of these pieces: name-based automatic mentions (`FEATURES.md:103`), proposed tags (`:104`), background AI tagging (`:105`), aliases (`:106`), scene summaries (`:115`), dated facts (`:190`) and the lookup ladder (`:133`). The research suggests merging tags into **entities with aliases**, where "mentions" are worked out by the app and never typed by the author.

## 5 ideas to steal

1. **Name and alias matching as the only way mentions are linked** (NovelCrafter, NovelAI, Scrivener's name search). It costs zero tokens and its results can be explained. It needs case-sensitive matching, an exclusion list and plural handling for common-word names ("Will", "May"). Manual tagging becomes unnecessary.
2. **Extract facts per scene, with a story-time date** (NovelCanon tier 2, NovelCrafter progressions automated, Graphiti's dated facts). The fast model runs once per finished scene and its result is reused until the scene's text changes. A fact the story later changes is marked out of date, never deleted. Context is built as of the scene being worked on, so later facts never leak into earlier scenes.
3. **Inclusion modes per entry, plus spoiler hiding per trait** (NovelCrafter's four modes; Sudowrite's hidden traits). The default is "when mentioned," and related entries are pulled in automatically.
4. **Consistency checks in tiers, with cost shown up front** (NovelCanon): free deterministic checks while typing, a cheap fast-model check per scene, and a whole-book scan only on demand that shows its cost estimate first.
5. **Show what was sent** (Sudowrite's Detection underline), and let the chat edit the bible only as proposals (Sudowrite "Allow edits"). Seeing what reached the AI builds trust and makes context bugs visible.

## 5 traps to avoid

1. **Upkeep the author must do by hand**, like NovelCrafter's codex or Scrivener's keywords. Reviewers call it the "time sink" every time.
2. **One-shot import or a re-sync the author has to trigger.** Sudowrite's import stopped after the first chapter and couldn't be re-run, and its bible drifts from the manuscript. Extraction must be incremental, safe to repeat, and re-run whenever a scene's text changes.
3. **Dumping the whole bible into every call** (Chronicle) **or dropping context silently when over budget** (SillyTavern's 25% default). Cost then grows with the bible, and accuracy quietly falls when entries are cut. Use a fixed budget, a clear priority order, and say what was left out.
4. **Using embeddings to decide which canon applies.** SillyTavern's own docs call vector activation unpredictable. Keep embeddings for finding passages, not for choosing which facts reach the AI.
5. **Treating AI analysis as authoritative.** Evidence: ProWritingAid's false flags, Fictionary's Cratchit/Cratchits split, NovelCanon's weakness on implicit contradictions, Storysnap's chapter-by-chapter blindness, and the warning that "a wrong fact in the recap becomes a wrong fact in the next chapter." Every AI fact should cite its passage and be marked as observed by the AI. It must never overwrite a fact the author entered, and a contradiction should cite both passages.

A sixth worth noting: **credit systems people can't see into.** Sudowrite's credits are the most common complaint about it, so show the cost per action.
