## Needs, ranked by how often they come up and how strongly writers feel them

### 1. Quick recall of small facts: eye colour, names, minor characters, who died (most frequent)
- Series authors keep bibles mainly for "character ages, traits, habits, hobbies; setting details" ([Spann Craig](https://elizabethspanncraig.com/uncategorized/keeping-track-of-what-weve-written/)). One commenter there said the "search function in word is incredibly helpful for making sure I haven't given a character two different names." Another author lists "every minor character mentioned by name, marking whether they died" ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/16040878-keeping-track-of-small-details-in-a-long-novel)).
- A character can be blond in chapter 1 and brunette in chapter 10, because "earlier drafts often linger in a writer's memory" ([Pattison](https://www.darcypattison.com/?p=5950)). Readers notice ([Goodreads reader thread](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/12076007-possible-mistakes-about-the-physical-description-of-the-characters)).
- At professional scale this is a paid job. Sanderson's continuity editor keeps an internal wiki so "Brandon does not contradict himself," plus a master timeline spreadsheet ([Coppermind](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Karen_Ahlstrom)).
- The standard fix today is manual: "list subjects that might be inconsistent… run manuscript searches (redhead, car, kitchen…)" ([CareerAuthors](https://careerauthors.com/5-ways-to-avoid-continuity-gaffes/)).

### 2. Keeping the bible up to date (the main reason writers give up on it)
- "If the friction of using it exceeds the pain of not using it, you stop reaching for it." A bible kept in a separate app costs a context switch every time it is opened ([Novela](https://novela.so/en/blog/story-bible-that-gets-used)). One novelist builds hers only after the draft, because "stopping to scrawl notes during the drafting stage slows me down" (same search).
- A bible that is never updated is "a liability… creates the illusion of a reliable reference while silently falling out of sync with your draft." Challenge guides schedule a "Weekly Story Bible Check Up." An Obsidian user admits keeping theirs updated "doesn't always happen" ([ProseEngine](https://proseengine.app/blog/what-is-a-story-bible-for-ai-writing-and-how-to-build-one-from-your-draft), [FirstDraft60](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/9189397-firstdraft60-day-37-weekly-story-bible-check-up)).
- NovelCrafter's Codex is called "powerful but a time sink" because every entry is typed by hand ([Medium review](https://ilampadmanabhan.medium.com/novelcrafter-review-powerful-for-fiction-writers-frustrating-to-set-up-april-2026-64d391c629a2), [DreamGen](https://dreamgen.com/blog/articles/novelcrafter-review)). NovelCrafter's own course warns: "Outdated summaries can lead to inconsistencies or invented details" ([NovelCrafter](https://www.novelcrafter.com/courses/ultimate-beginners-guide/scene-summaries-and-the-writing-loop)).
- A Sudowrite user asks for extraction from the manuscript, noting it "did not continue doing so as I added additional chapters" ([Sudowrite feedback](https://feedback.sudowrite.com/p/story-bible-and-editing-enhancements)).
- Templates breed setup fatigue: World Anvil "quickly became tedious most steps of the way" ([Kismet](https://www.dnd.kismetrose.com/DMWorldAnvil.html)). Writers name "worldbuilder's disease" and advise building only what the scenes need ([TCK](https://www.tckpublishing.com/worldbuilders-disease/)).

### 3. Time, ages, who was where, and who knows what (very strong for mystery, epic fantasy and series)
- A common spreadsheet layout is character names across the top and dates down the side ([L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/on-scrivener-storymill-and-the-value-of-timelines/3359?page=2)). Aeon Timeline users value automatic age calculation ([SleuthSayers](https://www.sleuthsayers.org/2023/02/plotting-your-story-with-aeon-timeline.html)).
- One novelist's parallel timelines were "way out of sync" and needed a rewrite, which they traced to not starting a timeline early ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21471108-out-of-sync)).
- Mystery writers stress that "your protagonist can only act on information she/he has," a problem that gets worse with more viewpoints ([Fictionary](https://fictionary.co/journal/elements-of-a-mystery)). Wheel of Time fans built a crowd-sourced "who knows what" database ([Dragonmount](https://dragonmount.com/forums/topic/50749-who-knows-what-collaborative-database/)).
- NovelCrafter's "progressions" (facts that only apply from a given scene onward, so earlier scenes see no spoilers) show the market wants facts tied to a point in the story ([docs](https://www.novelcrafter.com/help/docs/codex/progressions-codex-details)).

### 4. "Where did I mention X?" and which scenes a character is in
- "Scrivener can't tell which words are character names." The suggested fix is a keyword per character, applied to every document ([L&L](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/finding-character-names-within-my-book/37992)).
- One user found separate "Char Name – On" and "Char Name – Off" keywords for every character "very cumbersome" ([L&L](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/tracking-characters-how-do-you-do-it/128018)). **Tagging by hand is the chore writers complain about.**
- Reviewers single out NovelCrafter's automatic name and alias detection as its standout feature ([Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/novelcrafter-review/)). Obsidian novelists prefer links that are created and updated automatically over tags, and use tags only to filter (e.g. main vs side character) ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/best-practices-for-tracking-characters-locations-and-timeline-etc-in-a-novel/33990)).

### 5. Trust in facts the AI produces (intense, and growing with AI tools)
- When a bible is vague, the AI fills the gaps with invented details, and writers can accept them without noticing ([Sudowrite review](https://www.rivalflow.com/blog/sudowrite-review-ai-writing-tool-help-write-a-novel)). An aggregator of about 681 Sudowrite app reviews cites "inconsistent AI memory and hallucination" ([Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/apps/sudowrite-ai-novel-writing)).
- AI continuity checking is "assistive, not authoritative: it hallucinates connections" ([Inkfluence](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/best-ai-novel-continuity-checking-2026)). The recommended pattern is that "canon exists only after approval," and the test of a tool is whether "rejected details stay rejected" ([ShakespeareAI](https://shakespeareai.net/blog/ai-story-bible-generator-for-novels)).
- Uploading a whole book and asking an AI about it is unreliable. On r/WritingWithAI one user said the AI "skims and guesses" ([mirror](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/WritingWithAI/comments/1leojc3/an_ai_strength_for_writing)), and another thread reports coherence degrading past about 100k tokens ([mirror](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rb65g7/snapshots/2026-02-22T02%3A21%3A26.610158Z)).
- Pure keyword triggers miss indirect references: "the wound" does not fire the "scar" entry, so the AI invents one ([NovelAI docs](https://docs.novelai.net/en/text/lorebook); the example is from a competitor's page).

### 6. Where writers accept AI: notes and lookup yes, prose no
- Among the 45% of BookBub-surveyed authors who use generative AI, the top uses are research (81%) and outlining (72%). Only about 11% of fiction authors reportedly generate publishable text with it (that figure is secondary and unverified). The Authors Guild 2025 survey reportedly found 87% do not use generative AI in their writing process ([Authorlytica](https://authorlytica.com/state-of-author-ai/), [manuscriptreport](https://manuscriptreport.com/data/ai-publishing-statistics)).
- So AI is accepted as a clerk and librarian. It is contested as a co-author.

### 7. Storyboarding: most writers are hybrids, and outlines drift
- A pantser's bible is "a log of details invented during writing," which matters more for them than for plotters ([Writeo](https://www.writeo.app/blogs/how-to-build-a-story-bible)). Outlines get abandoned "the second they started typing." The common fix is an "as-is" reverse outline after the draft ([Dunsmore](https://madelinemdunsmore.substack.com/p/authors-notes-on-honey-i-broke-the), [Dabble](https://dabblewriter.com/articles/fyfd-day-three)).
- Card boards (Plottr) are valued for filtering scenes by POV, location and character ([Reedsy](https://blog.reedsy.com/plottr-review)).

### 8. Trying things out without polluting canon
- Scrivener snapshots let "only one version 'live' at a time" and cannot be searched, so writers keep "Alternate Scenes" folders ([L&L](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/best-practices-for-efficiently-saving-alternate-scenes/133587)).
- Sudowrite's brainstorm is valued for options: one tester got "8 alternative plot paths" ([NichePursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/?p=130141)). Loom's branching "multiverse" of continuations is an earlier, experimental take on the same idea ([Loom](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1841143)).
- "The story bible is for facts you've committed to, not possibilities you're exploring" (search excerpt, source not pinned among [Seabell](https://www.seabell.com/blog/story-bible-template) and similar guides).

### 9. Cost
- NovelCrafter's own example: 9 codex entries (643 words) made a prompt of about 4,176 input tokens. Entries set to "always include" are paid for on every call ([NovelCrafter BYOK](https://docs.novelcrafter.com/en/articles/8695405-how-much-does-the-ai-the-models-cost)).

## What this means for a low-upkeep design

1. **The manuscript is the only thing the author maintains.** The bible should be a derived, rebuildable index of facts, not a second document to keep in sync. Every fact carries its source (scene plus a quoted span) and the scene it holds from, so the app can answer "as of chapter 12." This answers needs 2, 3 and 5.
2. **Drop manual tagging as the way things get found. Detect entities automatically.** Name and alias matching runs locally and costs no tokens. MythScribe already has this in `findMentions` (`src/shared/mentions.ts:98`), plus F-4.12 automatic mentions and F-4.12b proposed tags. Use the fast AI tier only for what plain matching cannot settle: indirect references ("the wound"), pronouns, and new names. Keep optional labels for themes and plot threads, but nothing should depend on them. Hand tagging is the most-cited chore and the reason Scrivener keywords get abandoned.
3. **Two levels of facts, and review only the exceptions.** Facts the author confirmed always win. Facts the AI observed show their quote and can be confirmed or rejected in one click, and a rejection sticks. Ask the author only about conflicts and new entities, never "review all facts." This keeps both trust and upkeep low.
4. **Lookups answer from the index first.** Run full-text and mention search locally. The AI writes the answer from a few cited passages, never the whole book (whole-book reading "skims and guesses"). "Not found" is a valid answer.
5. **Time and knowledge come from the same extraction.** When summarizing a scene, also pull out the date or relative time, who is present, and what each character learns. Ages and "who knows what" can then be worked out by code, with no extra AI calls.
6. **The storyboard is a live reverse outline.** Generate cards from the scenes (summary, POV, place, cast), let the author edit them, and flag planned cards the draft has drifted from. This works for pantsers and plotters alike.
7. **Sandboxes are separate from canon.** Alternate scene versions and what-if chats stay out of the fact index until the author promotes them.
8. **Spend tokens only on changes.** Re-extract only scenes whose content hash changed. Batch extraction on the fast tier when the author pauses. Send only the facts for entities in the scene, never "always include" blobs. Run continuity checks on demand, against changed scenes only, on the strong tier.
