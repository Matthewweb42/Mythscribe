A desktop app for novelists

Write the book. It remembers the rest.

MythScribe keeps your story bible up to date while you write, flags the places where your book contradicts itself, and answers questions about your manuscript with the passage that proves it. Your manuscript stays on your computer.

See how it works

For Windows, macOS, and Linux. The editor, notes, and story bible work fully with AI switched off.

Feature tour

A writing room with a very good memory.

Everything a long book needs in one place: the manuscript, the people and places in it, and help that reads what you wrote before it says a word.

Write

A quiet page and a binder that bends to your book.

Parts, chapters, and scenes in a binder you can nest however your story is built, with word counts at every level. The page is a book-width column with your choice of type, a focus mode that hides everything else, and autosave on every pause.

  • Synopsis, notes, POV, location, and timeline beside every scene
  • Outline, timeline, goals, and writing statistics
  • Automatic backups, drafts, and snapshots you can compare

Ask your book

A chat that researches before it answers.

Ask where a character was last seen, what the reader knows by chapter ten, or whether a line sounds like the person saying it. The assistant searches your scenes, reads your character sheets, and answers with the passages it used. If it cannot find something, it says so.

  • Every answer cites a passage or one of your own sheets
  • You can watch each lookup as it happens
  • In Ask, a change it suggests waits for your Apply

Story bible

Worldbuilding that keeps itself.

Characters, places, and the rules of your world each get a sheet. As you write, MythScribe notes what each scene says about them (an age, a scar, who was where) and keeps it beside your own notes, marked as found in the manuscript. When a new scene disagrees with an earlier one, it tells you where.

  • Scene summaries and facts gathered in the background
  • Consistency checks that quote the passage in question
  • Import your existing sheets from JSON or a spreadsheet

Revise

Edit passes that come back as tracked changes.

Pick a kind of pass (developmental notes, a line edit, a copy edit, a proofread, a continuity pass, or your own instruction), pick the scenes, and see the cost in dollars before you start. Every change lands in your text as a tracked change with a reason, for you to accept or reject one by one.

  • Line and copy edits keep to your voice and your banned phrases
  • Names from your story bible are left alone
  • Every pass is saved as a report you can come back to

Your voice, your control

You decide how much help you get.

AI is off when you install. Turn on Use AI for a project and pick how the assistant may act, right under its message box. Change it whenever you like.

  • Auto

    Looks things up, answers with sources, and makes its edits itself, each with Undo. Anything that deletes still asks first.

  • Ask Recommended

    Looks things up and answers with sources. Every edit it wants to make waits for your Apply; nothing changes without you.

  • Plan

    Reads the whole project to discuss and brainstorm with you. It never proposes or makes an edit.

  • Local-first

    A project is a folder on your disk. No account, no sync you did not ask for, and it works offline. Copy the folder and you have a backup.

  • Bring your own key

    Use your own OpenAI key, kept in your system’s secure storage, or a model running on your own machine. Requests go straight to the provider, never through us.

  • Nothing hidden

    Each feature lists exactly what it sends, and the AI settings page reproduces that list word for word. Every request shows its cost, and a daily cap stops spending at a number you choose.

FAQ

Questions writers ask

Where is my writing stored, and who can see it?

On your computer. A project is a folder holding one database file and your images; nothing is uploaded and there is no account. With AI off, nothing leaves your machine. With AI on, a feature sends only the text it needs, straight to the provider you chose, and the AI settings page lists exactly what each one sends. The privacy policy has the details.

Do I have to use AI?

No. MythScribe installs with AI off, and the binder, editor, notes, story bible, tags, outline, timeline, and backups all work without it. You can switch AI on for one project and leave it off for another.

How much does the AI cost?

With your own OpenAI key you pay OpenAI directly; for a writer working every day that is roughly a few dollars a month (see the estimate). The app shows the cost of every request and a running total, and stops at a daily cap you set. A model running on your own computer costs nothing.

What do Auto, Ask, and Plan mean?

They are the assistant’s modes, picked under its message box. In Auto it makes its edits itself, each with Undo, and still asks before deleting anything. In Ask every edit waits for your Apply. In Plan it only reads and talks; it never edits. Edit passes always come back as tracked changes, whatever the mode.

Can I bring in a manuscript and my worldbuilding notes?

Yes. Import a Word (.docx) or Markdown file and MythScribe finds the chapters and scenes; you review and adjust them before anything is created. Character, place, and world sheets import from a JSON file or a spreadsheet (CSV), and export the same way, so you can reuse them across books.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Writing, organising, search, and backups need no connection. AI features need to reach your provider, unless you run a local model on your own computer.

Which platforms does it run on?

Windows, macOS, and Linux. MythScribe is in development and there are no public downloads yet; the buttons on this page will work once the first signed builds are out.
